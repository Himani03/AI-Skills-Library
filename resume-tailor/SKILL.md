# Resume tailoring

Use this whenever Himani asks to tailor, edit, tighten, or build a resume for a job description, role type, or track.

## Non-negotiables

- **Accuracy first.** Every claim, metric, tool, title, and date must be backed by something she told you, her saved notes, past conversations, or her current resume. Never invent or stretch. If a number or skill is unverified, ask her or leave it out. When something can't be claimed, offer a real alternative (adjacent real experience, or a project she could build).
- **Check before flagging a gap.** Look through her saved notes, project list, and earlier conversations for verified experience before saying she lacks something. Much of it is already on record.
- **Be direct.** Give honest ratings and gap assessments. No flattery, no inflating a score to hit a target.
- **Surgical edits by default.** Change only what the job description makes worth changing. Rebuild from scratch only if she explicitly asks.
- **Don't change** employers, job titles, or dates unless she says to. Ask if unsure.

## Format (ATS rules, every time)

- One page, strict. Convert to PDF and count pages to confirm.
- Output as .docx (use the docx skill to build it).
- 0.5 inch margins on all four sides.
- Calibri throughout. Body around 10 pt. If it spills over, tighten spacing and trim content before going below 9.5 pt.
- Section headings with a navy divider line (for example 1F3864).
- No em dashes anywhere. Use commas, colons, or parentheses instead. Use a plain hyphen for date ranges.
- Contact line uses email shahhimani3546@gmail.com. Hyperlink every contact detail (mailto for the email, plus her LinkedIn linkedin.com/in/himani-shah03 and GitHub github.com/Himani03). Copy phone and location from her latest resume, never guess them.
- Bullets follow XYZ format: what she accomplished, measured by what, by doing what. One to two lines each.
- Vary opening verbs. Don't open two bullets with the same verb if you can avoid it, and skip weak openers like "Responsible for", "Worked on", "Helped".

## Workflow

1. **Get inputs.** The job description (pasted text or URL, fetch the page if it's a link) and the base resume. If she didn't attach one, use the closest earlier tailored version from the conversation, otherwise ask.
2. **Fit check first.** Before editing, give a rating out of 10 with the top two to four gaps. Say which gaps are real and which are just framing. If she's aiming for a 9/10, say what would honestly get there and what wouldn't.
3. **Edit surgically.** Summary line, skills ordering, bullet rewrites that bring the most relevant real work forward, and project swaps. Leave the rest untouched.
4. **Projects.** Always swap in her strongest-matching projects for this JD, pulled from her real portfolio. Don't list a project as finished if it is only planned or not built yet. If none of her projects fit well, say so and suggest one specific project idea she can build (what it is, stack, what it proves, rough time to build). Keep it off the resume until it exists.
5. **Keywords.** Use the JD's terms only where they are true for her, worked in the way she would naturally say it. No mirroring JD sentences, no keyword stuffing, no tools she hasn't used. If the JD names a tool she's missing, flag it as a gap.
6. **Voice.** Bullets should read like a real person wrote them: plain words, concrete details. Avoid corporate and AI-sounding filler (seamless, leverage, spearheaded, robust, cutting-edge, passionate, synergy).
7. **Verify before sending.** One page, margins, font, no em dashes (search the text), links work, numbers and dates match their source.
8. **Deliver.** Send the .docx. In the reply, keep it short: the fit rating, what changed in a few lines, remaining real gaps, and a project idea if one applies.
